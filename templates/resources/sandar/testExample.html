Step 3

3 The Midpoint ($M$)

The midpoint is the precise center of our segment. It is calculated as the average of the x-coordinates and the average of the y-coordinates.

Step 4

4 Gradient calculation ($m$)

The gradient represents the slope, or steepness, of a line. Mathematically, it is the change in vertical coordinates (rise) divided by the change in horizontal coordinates (run).

Step 5

5 Finding the y-intercept ($c$)

The y-intercept represents the point where our line crosses the vertical y-axis ($x = 0$). Mathematically, we substitute our gradient $m$ and any coordinate $(x, y)$ into $y = mx + c$ to solve for $c$.

Step 6

6 Parallel lines

Lines are parallel if they run in the exact same direction and never intersect. This means **parallel lines share the exact same gradient ($m$)** but cross the y-axis at a different value ($c_p$).
