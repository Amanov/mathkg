# CPA Design: Introducing the Four Operations Conceptually

Singapore-style Concrete → Pictorial → Abstract sequence, delivered through a 5E lesson cycle, checked through a 3C lens, and mapped to Bloom's revised taxonomy.

**Audience:** Grades 1–2 (age 7–8), the "number bonds, place value via CPA" band in `docs/curriculum-framework.md` §4. Formal written algorithms are deliberately out of scope; this unit builds the *meaning* of each operation.

**Status:** Design draft for review. Two things need an owner decision (see §9): what "3C" means for MathKG, and native-speaker review of the Kyrgyz terms.

---

## 1. Design principles

1. **Meaning before procedure.** A child can say what × *means* (equal groups) before being asked to recall 6 × 7.
2. **One idea, three representations.** Every operation is met as objects → bar model/drawing → number sentence, and the child must move *between* them in both directions.
3. **Structure first, then stories.** Each operation is introduced through its *structure* (part-whole, equal groups) and only then through the several story types that share it.
4. **Operations are related.** Addition ↔ subtraction and multiplication ↔ division are taught as fact families from day one, not as four separate chapters.
5. **Language is mathematics.** Kyrgyz agglutinative terms (кошуу, кемитүү, көбөйтүү, бөлүү and their results) are used consistently from the first lesson (curriculum-framework §5.3).

---

## 2. The three frameworks, and how they fit together

| Framework | Role in this design | Operates at |
|---|---|---|
| **CPA** (Singapore) | *What the child handles*: objects, then pictures/bar models, then symbols | Across the unit and inside each lesson |
| **5E** | *How the lesson flows*: Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate | Each lesson (45 min) |
| **3C** (assumed: Concept · Context · Communication; see §9) | *Quality check on every lesson*: is the idea clear, is it tied to a real situation, can the child say it? | Lesson design checklist |
| **Bloom (revised)** | *Cognitive demand*: Remember → Create | Every activity is tagged with a level |

**How CPA and 5E interlock.** CPA is not a strict three-lesson ladder. Inside one 5E lesson the child touches all three stages, with the weight shifting:

| 5E phase | Minutes | Dominant CPA stage | Bloom level targeted |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Engage** | 5 | Concrete (story + real objects) | Remember / Understand |
| **Explore** | 12 | Concrete (hands-on, child-led, teacher questions) | Understand / Apply |
| **Explain** | 10 | Pictorial → Abstract (bar model, then number sentence, vocabulary introduced *after* experience) | Understand / Analyze |
| **Elaborate** | 10 | Abstract with pictorial support; new contexts | Apply / Analyze |
| **Evaluate** | 8 | All three, tested separately (see §6) | Evaluate / Create |

Across the *unit*, early lessons are concrete-heavy and later lessons are abstract-heavy, but no lesson drops the concrete entirely until the child chooses to.

## 3. Conceptual map of the four operations

| Operation | Core structure | Story types (taught in this order) | Bar model | Key vocabulary (Kyrgyz) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Addition** (кошуу) | Part + Part = Whole | 1. Joining (add to) 2. Combining (two groups) | Two parts under one whole bar | кошулуучу, сумма |
| **Subtraction** (кемитүү) | Whole − Part = Other part | 1. Taking away 2. Finding the missing part 3. Comparing (difference) | Whole bar with one part shaded; two bars side by side for comparison | кемитилүүчү, кемиткич, айырма |
| **Multiplication** (көбөйтүү) | Equal groups: groups × size = total | 1. Equal groups 2. Repeated addition 3. Arrays (rows × columns) | Several equal units in one bar | көбөйтүүчү, көбөйтүндү |
| **Division** (бөлүү) | Total ÷ groups = size *or* total ÷ size = groups | 1. Sharing (how many in each group?) 2. Grouping (how many groups?) | Total bar split into equal units | бөлүнүүчү, бөлгүч, тийинди |

**Cross-links, all explicit in the lessons:**
- Number bond ↔ fact family: 3, 4, 7 → 3+4=7, 4+3=7, 7−3=4, 7−4=3.
- Repeated addition bridges + to ×; repeated subtraction/sharing bridges − to ÷.
- Array turned 90° shows commutativity (3×4 = 4×3).
- Division is *inverse* of multiplication: 12 ÷ 3 = 4 because 3 × 4 = 12.

## 4. Unit sequence (4 units, 5 lessons each, 20 lessons)

Each unit is one full pass of CPA: lessons 1–2 concrete-led, 3 pictorial-led, 4 abstract-led, 5 consolidation and assessment. The 5E cycle runs inside each lesson (§5).

## 5. One lesson in full: model template (Lesson C1, Equal Groups)

Written once as the template content authors copy for the other 19 lessons.

**Objective (child-facing):** "I can tell when groups are equal and make equal groups."
**Concept · Context · Communication (3C):**
- *Concept:* equal groups (same number in each group)
- *Context:* serving plates of plov / bread at a family dastarkhan
- *Communication:* "There are 3 plates with 4 pieces of bread on each plate."

| Phase | What happens | Teacher question | Bloom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Engage** (5) | Teacher tells a 60-second story: Aigul sets 3 plates and puts bread on them, but one plate has more. Child reaction: "That is not fair!" | "What is wrong? What would you do?" | Remember / Understand |
| **Explore** (12) | Pairs get plates and counters. Task 1: make 3 plates with the same number. Task 2: find two *different* ways with 12 counters. Task 3: sort teacher-made cards into "equal" / "not equal". | "How do you know they are equal? Can you prove it without counting everything?" | Apply |
| **Explain** (10) | Class shares findings. Teacher draws what pairs built (circles with dots), introduces the phrase "3 groups of 4", then the bar model: one bar of 3 equal boxes labelled 4. | "Where do you see the 3? Where do you see the 4? What does the whole bar mean?" | Understand / Analyze |
| **Elaborate** (10) | New contexts: wheels on 3 bicycles (2 each), fingers on 2 hands, eggs in trays. Child picks counters *or* draws *or* writes "3 groups of 4". | "Which of these are equal groups? Which are not? Make up your own." | Apply / Analyze |
| **Evaluate** (8) | Three-part exit ticket (§6) | — | Evaluate / Create |

**Common misconceptions to watch for:** counting objects instead of groups ("12" when asked how many groups); treating unequal groups as valid; confusing "3 groups of 4" with "4 groups of 3" (acceptable here, and a lead-in to commutativity in C3).

## 6. Assessment: CPA-separated, Bloom-leveled

Each unit's Lesson 5 exit ticket tests the three representations separately, so a teacher can see *where* a child is stuck.

| Item | Representation tested | Bloom level | Example (Unit C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Concrete**: do it | Apply | "Make 3 groups of 2 with these counters." |
| 2 | **Pictorial**: read/draw | Understand / Analyze | "Draw the bar model for 4 plates with 3 apples each." |
| 3 | **Abstract**: symbols | Apply | "Write the number sentence for your picture." |
| 4 | **Translation**: move between forms | Analyze | "Which story matches 2 × 5? Which one does not?" |
| 5 | **Error analysis** | Evaluate | "Meerim wrote 3 × 4 = 7. What did she do? Explain." |
| 6 | **Create** | Create | "Make up a story for 12 ÷ 3. Draw it." |

**Mastery rule (proposed):** progress to the next unit when a child gets items 1–4 right; items 5–6 are extension evidence, not a gate.

### Bloom coverage across the unit

| Bloom level | Where it appears |
|---|---|
| Remember | Vocabulary, number bond facts, Engage recall |
| Understand | Explain phase, "what does this part of the bar mean?" |
| Apply | Explore tasks, Elaborate in new contexts |
| Analyze | Sort equal/unequal, compare sharing vs grouping, match story to model |
| Evaluate | Error analysis ("explain the mistake"), "which model is better?" |
| Create | Child writes own story for a given number sentence |

## 7. Differentiation

- **Support:** stay concrete longer; ten-frames, pre-drawn bar templates; sentence stems in Kyrgyz ("... топто ... дан бар").
- **Extension:** missing-number and two-step stories; "Can you find a number that works for both × and ÷?"; fact-family puzzles.
- **Language:** each lesson ships a 4–6 term word bank with a picture for every term.

### Authoring checklist (every lesson)

- [ ] Starts from a real Kyrgyz context (dastarkhan, bazaar, yurt, school), not a bare number
- [ ] Concrete task uses objects available in a typical classroom (counters, stones, beans, cubes)
- [ ] Bar model shown before the symbol, never after
- [ ] Vocabulary introduced *after* the experience, then used consistently
- [ ] At least one Analyze-or-above item
- [ ] Exit ticket covers C, P, A separately

## 9. Open decisions

1. **What does "3C" mean here?** It is not a single universal standard. This draft assumes **Concept · Context · Communication** as a lesson-quality lens (§2, §5). If you intended another 3C (e.g. Connect–Communicate–Collaborate, or a framework used by your school or ministry), only the 3C rows in §2 and §5 change; the CPA/5E/Bloom structure is unaffected.
2. **Kyrgyz terminology.** Terms in §3 follow common textbook usage but need review by a native-speaking teacher against the current state-standard wording (see `docs/kyrgyzstan-state-standard-research.md`).
3. **Grade placement.** Written for grades 1–2. Because Kyrgyzstan is moving to a 12-year system (framework §4), confirm against the new standard's grade 1–2 outcomes before authoring.
4. **Pacing.** 20 lessons assumes 45-minute sessions; adjust to your teachers' actual timetable.
5. **Less, not more.** Per the framework's own warning (§3 tension note), authoring this unit should *replace* some worksheet-first content rather than add to it.
